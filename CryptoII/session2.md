- The lifetime chance of an asteroid or comet impacting Earth with catastrophic regional or global consequences is roughly 1 in 10,000, which translates to a yearly probability of about 1 in 300,000. On a second-by-second basis, the mathematical probability of a major ("killer") asteroid strike at any exact random second is virtually zero (on the order of roughly \(1.5 \times 10^{-13}\) to \(3 \times 10^{-13}\) per second).
- The exact chance of a random collision between two specific, different strings using SHA-256 is 1 in 2^256, which is approximately 1.15 × 10^77 (or a decimal starting with 76 zeros).
To put this number into perspective: • Fewer than atoms in the universe: The total number of unique SHA-256 hash outputs (2²⁵⁶ ≈ 1.15 × 10⁷⁷) is nearly equal to the estimated total number of atoms in the entire observable universe (≈ 10⁸⁰).
• Practically zero: The odds are so infinitesimally low that the possibility of a random collision is safely ignored in all real-world computing applications.

# Not my wallet, no my keys

Mt. Gox was a Tokyo bitcoin exchange that handled most of the world's BTC trading in the early 2010s. American programmer Jed McCaleb started it in 2010. In March 2011 he sold it to Mark Karpelès, a French developer. The name stood for "Magic: The Gathering Online eXchange".

How it collapsed
2011, an early warning: Hackers used a compromised auditor's computer to drop the exchange's bitcoin price to one cent. They then moved about 2,000 BTC out of customer accounts.

Feb. 7, 2014: Mt. Gox stopped all bitcoin withdrawals. It blamed "transaction malleability," a quirk in Bitcoin's software.

Feb. 24: Trading stopped and the website went offline. A leaked internal document said 744,408 BTC had been lost to a theft that went unnoticed for years.

Feb. 28: Mt. Gox filed for bankruptcy in Tokyo. It said 850,000 BTC were gone: 750,000 belonging to customers and 100,000 of its own, worth about $473 million at the time. That was roughly 7% of all bitcoins in existence. About $27.5 million in cash was missing too.

March 2014: The company found about 200,000 BTC in an old 2011-era wallet it had thought was empty. That left about 650,000 BTC missing.
