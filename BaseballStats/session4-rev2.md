## LLM-based AI  does not  consider  the  physiology, neurochemistry,  and emotions that make us human {#slide-6}

Decisions  ignoring that can  lead to clubhouse tensions, as Red Sox manager Chad Tracy found out

When pulled from the game after 70 pitches in a  recent  playoff loss to the Yankees, pitcher Sonny Gray said:  I would've liked to have been trusted in that moment. I think I'm the best pitcher in the American League going through the order the third time. I think that's a fact. I would have liked to live or die with me in that moment. I think you can probably ask the (other players), and they felt the same way.

His teammate Wilson Contreras:  We have to do a lot of different stuff and it's not enough when you have talent or you have data or you have computers telling you what to do .   This is baseball and the data is here to help us. But at the end of the day, you're going to need  to trust  experience and veteran guys to win games. There's a lot  more  stuff that I would love to say but I'll stay like that

Having very recently been awarded a new three-year contract, Tracy might have a tough time re-capturing the clubhouse next spring.

## LLM-based AI  does not  consider  the  physiology, neurochemistry,  and emotions that make us human  (cont.) {#slide-7}

Going back to Reggie Jackson (and probably before), we've used phrases like "comes through in the clutch", "wired differently", and "slows things down" to describe those great players who seem to rise to the occasion better than others.

It's not far-fetched to imagine an AI of the future  that's not LLM-based  and will consider these factors.   Its importance will be less about a manager's making statistically based decisions and more about how some athletes are better able to train their entire physiology into becoming "Reggie-Jackson-like".

Lucas Giolito  is a very good MLB pitcher who has successfully battled some arm health problems.  Even more significantly he learned how to use neurofeedback treatments to cope with anxiety that kept him from "slowing things down" in those pressure-packed moments that are so unique  to baseball.

## Milwaukee Brewers {#slide-15}

Roster Style: Young and athletic

Milwaukee began to put more emphasis on athleticism and contact hitting when it came to their position players, while encouraging situational awareness all through their system. This year\'s club is the most advanced manifestation of that approach.

On the pitching side, the Brewers are a mix of internally developed hurlers along with a number of acquired pitchers. Milwaukee has had an inordinate amount of success in coaxing better results from such pitchers.

Payroll Power: Bottom Third

The highest the Brewers have ranked in Opening Day payroll during their current 10-year run of success is 17th, back in 2019. They\'ve won four straight National League Central titles while ranking no higher than 20th

## Milwaukee Brewers {#slide-16}

Homegrown Percentage: 32%

The Brewers\' 40-man roster is 32% homegrown, ranking 20th; the 26-man roster is at 38%, the highest of the four remaining teams. The Brewers tend to acquire external players via trades and waiver claims as opposed to free agency.

Signature Signing: prospect extensions

The signings that have been in step with the Brewers\' philosophy have been extensions, especially to some of their most talented prospects. Three starters (Jackson Chourio, Cooper Pratt and Luis Lara) each signed extensions prior to reaching the majors.

Best Trade They've Made: Christian Yelich  

From Miami in January 2018 for four players.  He won NL MVP honors in his first season and finished second in the balloting the season after.  The four players the Brewers sent to Miami in the deal are all out of the majors and finished with a combined minus 5.9 career  bWAR .

## Milwaukee Brewers {#slide-17}

Biggest Reclamation Project Success - Jake Bauers 

This area is a specialty of the Brewers, so a typical season would see at least a couple of examples.  Before 2026, Bauers had a career OPS+ of 84 and was below replacement value (minus-1.2  bWAR ). This season, Bauers had a 140 OPS+, put up 3.3  bWAR  and led the Brewers with 26 home runs.

Historical Baggage: Overpacked

This is an esoteric way to describe the fact that the Brewers are one of five franchises never to win a title.
